# Writing for ajay.guru

This is Ajay’s own place to share what he learns and builds. Jobs are one part
of it. Films, questions and unfinished learning belong here too.

Start with his words. Ask him to explain something as he would to a friend,
then edit for clarity. Keep the details, the enthusiasm, the occasional
fragment and the small asides. Let one thought lead into the next instead of
turning every answer into a tidy little essay.

## Voice reference

> I love reading about directors and then watching their movies. Gives me a
> whole new perspective. Recently I watched Shoojit Sircar’s interview with
> Samdish and really liked how calm he was, how clearly he thought about
> things. You can see that in Piku. The story flows so naturally, but there’s
> so much attention to detail in the scenes and characters.

Ajay approved this sample because it stayed close to how he said it.

The railway story is another reference: fourth class, seeing a reservation
terminal, trying to build a reservation app in QBASIC the next week. It worked
without storage or persistence: “a glorified hello world.” Keep those details
and his “XD”; don’t turn it into an origin story about a lifelong mission.

## Editing

- Use contractions and let sentence lengths vary. Fragments are fine where
  they sound natural. Don’t chop every sentence into fragments.
- Keep real details. A director and a particular film say more than a list
  of hobbies. Explain work through what happened and what needed doing.
- Keep technical facts accurate. Use the existing work entries as the
  starting point; ask before adding achievements or personal anecdotes.
- Swearing, “haha” and “XD” are welcome when they come from Ajay. Don’t add
  them to manufacture personality or scatter them across every page.
- Allow uncertainty and difficulty. Don’t turn every experience into a
  lesson, a maxim or a claim about how he works.
- Avoid résumé language, forced modesty (“play chess badly”), ornamental
  phrases (“a calmness that stayed with me”) and generic invitations.
- Read a paragraph aloud. If it sounds like someone describing Ajay’s brand,
  go back to the original answer.

## Content boundaries from the interview

- The Nango idea stays off the site. It is only a thought, not a project.
- There are no personal projects to list yet. Add them when Ajay has
  something he wants to share; no placeholders or invented projects.
- Keep specific complaints about coworkers out of public copy. His own
  experience of learning to lead a team can be discussed candidly.
- Keep professional roles and dates grounded in the existing content.
